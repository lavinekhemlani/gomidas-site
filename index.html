Straight answers

The five questions everyone asks.

What exactly do I own?
A fraction of one royalty on one named mine, held in its own segregated vehicle. Not shares in the mine. Not a loan. A registered right to a slice of everything it sells.
What happens if the mine stops producing?
Payments stop — nothing accrues and nothing is owed. The claim stays registered against the licence, so a restart brings it back. A mine can pause for years. Size your position knowing that.
How do I sell?
Your fraction is a token that transfers to any other verified holder, any hour. Early on the market will be thin — buy only what you'd be comfortable holding.
Why is this on-chain at all?
Because settlement in minutes beats a registrar queue, and automated pro-rata payouts beat chasing cheques. The royalty is legal machinery; the token is just how it moves.
Who verifies the mine isn't lying?
Independent engineers and geologists review the reserve and production data before anything lists — and the full file publishes with the instrument, so you can check their work.

Salum Salt — the project brief.

One of Africa's largest planned solar saltworks: a 7,724-hectare Atlantic coastal site at Dakhla–Oued Ed Dahab, Morocco, building in phases to 1.5 million tonnes of high-grade sea salt a year. Feedstock is seawater. Energy is sun and wind. Neither sends an invoice.

Atlantic-coast solar saltworksrepresentative image
The site

7,724 ha of flat coastal salt flats on the Imlili flats — salt crystallizes there naturally today. A topographic survey confirms 88% usable, with 1,300 ha of reserve beyond the full plan. ~30mm of rain a year, constant wind, year-round evaporation.

The chemistry

Independent lab analysis confirms the intake and subsurface brines are optimal: sodium-chloride enriched, magnesium and sulfate naturally depleted. Washed product specs at ~99.5% NaCl — chlor-alkali and food grade.

The infrastructure

A new $1.2B deep-water port (Dakhla Atlantique, cargo from 2028) and a 1,650-ha industrial zone are rising next door, with Morocco's strongest tax incentives on qualifying industrial projects.

What is a royalty?

A royalty is a fixed share of everything a mine sells — every tonne, every ounce — paid to the royalty holder before costs, banks and shareholders. It's written against the mine's licence, so it survives a sale, a refinancing, even a bankruptcy. It isn't a loan. It isn't a share. It's older and simpler than both.

The comparison

Royalty vs equity vs debt.

Three ways to own a piece of a mine's future. They are not the same animal.

Equity (shares)Debt (bonds/loans)Royalty

PaidLast — if anything is leftOn schedule — until defaultFirst — off the top line

The catch, stated plainly: a royalty only pays if the mine produces and sells. No production, no payment — sometimes for years. That's the risk you're paid a premium to hold.

Why the numbers get that big

You buy a share of the ground. Not a share of the plan.

Those multiples are not luck, and they are not leverage. They come from two things that are built into how a royalty works — and both of them only ever point one way.

Every ounce found later is free to you

A royalty is written against the licence area, not against the mine plan. When Goldstrike was bought it was a small heap-leach operation. The buyer paid for what was known. Then roughly 50 million ounces were found underneath it — and the 4% was paid on all of it, without a second cheque. Detour Lake was bought in 1998 and is still being extended today, heading to a million ounces a year into the 2040s.

A higher gold price makes the mine bigger, not just richer

When the price rises, ore that was too poor to touch becomes worth mining. Cut-off grades drop, reserves grow, and the mine's life gets longer. So a royalty holder earns more per ounce, on more ounces, for more years — three effects from one move. That is why a royalty book runs at more than 100% leverage to the metal price while carrying none of the cost.

And your cost never moves

The royalty is a fixed share of revenue, paid before costs. Diesel doubles, labour strikes, the mill needs replacing, the operator raises money at a terrible price — none of it touches the cheque. The company that bought Goldstrike outright paid 31 times more than the royalty holder did, and carried forty years of capital and operating risk to do it. The royalty holder just got paid.

01The platform
01.01What is Midas?

A market for mining royalties. We underwrite royalties on producing and near-producing mines, register them against the mining licence, split them into fractions, and let verified investors buy, hold and trade them — from $500.

01.02What problem does it solve?

Mines wait months for capital and give up equity to get it. Investors have never been able to buy a royalty at all — the whole $60B+ sector is held by three companies and a handful of institutions. We connect the two directly.

01.03Who can use it?

Investors who pass identity and suitability checks, mine operators raising against production, and existing royalty holders who want liquidity. Some jurisdictions we simply can't onboard yet — we'll tell you at signup, not after.

02Royalties & fractions
02.01What exactly am I buying?

A registered fraction of one named royalty — a fixed share of everything one specific mine sells, for the life of that mine. Each royalty sits in its own segregated vehicle. Your token is the register entry for your fraction. Never a pooled fund, never a basket.

02.02How is this different from buying mining stocks?

A share is a claim on profits — after costs, debt, taxes and management. A royalty is a claim on revenue, paid before all of that. Costs can double and your cheque doesn't change. Equity gets diluted; a royalty percentage is written into the licence and can't be.

02.03How do I get paid?

The mine reports sales, the royalty is calculated on revenue, and distributions land in your account — typically quarterly. You'll see the production data behind every payment.

02.04What's the minimum?

$500 on open deals. Anchor tranches for institutions start in the hundreds of thousands.

03For miners
03.01How does this raise capital for my mine?

You sell a royalty — a small fixed share of future revenue — instead of equity or debt. Cash up front, no board seat, no covenants, no dilution, no repayment schedule. We underwrite, paper and distribute the raise.

03.02Does it dilute my equity?

No. A royalty touches your revenue line, not your cap table. Every share of your company is still yours.

03.03Can I buy my royalty back?

Buy-back windows can be written into the deal at issue. If you want that option, we structure it up front, in the open, priced in the terms.

03.04What projects are eligible?

Producing or clearly-dated near-production assets, licence in good standing, a real reserve report (JORC / NI 43-101), and enforceable registration in your jurisdiction. Exploration-stage projects: not yet — that's a different risk we don't sell.

04Buying & trading
04.01How do I buy?

Create an account, pass verification, fund, and take an allocation on an open deal — or bid on fractions other holders are selling once secondary trading opens for that instrument.

04.02Can I sell?

Fractions transfer between verified holders on the platform. Early on, liquidity will be thin — treat a royalty as an income asset you can exit, not a day-trade. We say this everywhere on purpose.

04.03What are the fees?

Issuance fees are charged to the raise and disclosed in every deal file. Trading and distribution fees are published on the fee schedule before you commit a dollar. No fee you haven't seen in writing.

05Technology & custody
05.01Is my money held by Midas?

Client funds and instruments are held through segregated structures — one vehicle per royalty — separate from Midas's own balance sheet. The exact custody arrangement is published per deal.

05.02Why tokens at all?

Because a register that settles in minutes, splits to any size, and can't lose a certificate is simply better plumbing than a PDF and a spreadsheet. The token is the record of your fraction — the legal claim it represents is what you're buying.

05.03Can Midas move my assets?

No. Transfers happen only on your instruction, between verified holders. That restriction is structural, not a policy.

What the market pays

Why gold is cheap and copper isn't.

Royalties don't have one price. Buyers pay up for certainty and demand a discount for risk, and the spread between the two is where the whole business lives.

Gold, producing5–7%

The deepest buyer pool on earth. Everyone wants it, so it prices richest — meaning the yield is lowest.

Silver & precious, producing7–10%

Same logic, thinner crowd. A step up in yield for a step down in familiarity.

Copper & base metals, producing12–17%

The majors are built on gold. Base metals get less attention and cost more to fund — which is exactly why the yield is higher.

Construction & development stage15–35%

Nothing is being sold yet. You're paid for waiting and for the chance it never starts — the highest yields on the platform live here.

No price, because we don't do it. No production, no reserve, no royalty. This is where the money goes to die.

The five levers

What moves your number.

Is it producing?

A mine already selling metal pays you next quarter. One still being built pays you when it starts — if it starts. That gap is most of the yield difference in the table above.

Which metal

Gold has the deepest bid and the lowest yield. Copper, lithium and tin pay you more precisely because fewer people are competing for them.

Where it sits

The same royalty is worth less where the licence is harder to enforce. Jurisdiction is priced, every time, and it is priced honestly.

How long it lasts

Reserves set the floor, not the ceiling. Mines that keep drilling keep paying — Goldstrike has been paying on a 1986 royalty for nearly four decades.

The honest part

What can go wrong, in plain words.

The mine slows or stops

Payments pause with it. Nothing accrues, nothing is owed. The claim survives and pays again on restart — but a mine can sit idle for years.

The metal price falls

Your royalty is a share of revenue, so it falls too. You're paid off the top rather than out of profit, which is why royalties survive downturns that wipe out equity — but the cheque still shrinks.

You want out early

Royalties are illiquid. Selling before a buyer wants it means selling at their price. Size your position on the assumption you hold it.

You can lose everything you put into a royalty. We put the risks on the same page as the returns because anyone who doesn't is selling you something.

The obvious question

You can. Here is exactly what you get when you do — and what you give up.

You pay for the wrapper

The listed royalty companies have historically traded well above the appraised value of their books — the market charges a premium for access. Buy the stream directly and you pay what the asset is worth, priced against the reserve report, not the share register.

You buy their whole book

A share of a royalty company is 400+ assets, management's next acquisition, their G&A, and their cost of capital — averaged. You cannot pick the one royalty you actually want. Here, one instrument is one named asset with one file. Underwrite it yourself and disagree with us.

You inherit equity beta

Royalty stocks trade with the equity market — drawdowns hit the share price even when the underlying cheques never miss. A direct royalty pays what the mine sells, and its income doesn't care what the S&P did this week.

Risk

Named risks. Named answers.

R.01What if the mine slows or stops?

Payments pause with production — that's the deal. We list producing assets first, the claim survives a pause and pays again on restart, and every file carries sizing guidance so no one bets the house on one pit.

R.02What if the metal price falls?

Distributions shrink — a royalty tracks revenue. But it's paid off the top, before costs, so it keeps paying through downturns that kill the operator's profit. There is no leverage inside any vehicle to turn a bad year into a wipeout.

R.03What if the rules change where the mine sits?

Every royalty is registered against the mining licence in the host jurisdiction, with a local counsel enforceability opinion before it lists. Jurisdiction risk is priced, not ignored — it's why tier-one assets yield less.

R.04What if the mine is sold, refinanced, or mismanaged?

The claim attaches to the licence, not the owner. It survives every change of hands — sale, refinancing, most bankruptcies. Goldstrike's royalty has outlived three ownership regimes. We monitor operators monthly.

R.05Who actually holds my asset — can it be moved?

One segregated SPV per royalty; the token is the register entry. Transfers clear only between verified holders, and only on your instruction. Midas cannot move your position.

R.06Can I lose everything?

Yes. Royalty interests are illiquid and a mine can fail before paying back your capital. Nothing on this platform removes that — our job is to make every risk legible before you commit, not to pretend it away.
