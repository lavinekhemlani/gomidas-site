The instrument

Three ways to fund a mine. Two of them hurt.

Option one

Equity

  • Permanent dilution, priced at the worst moment in the cycle
  • Sells the upside on every tonne, forever
  • Cheapest when you don't need it
Option two

Debt

  • Interest accrues whether the mine runs or not
  • Covenants, security, a repayment date
  • One bad quarter is a default
Option three

A royalty

  • No interest. No covenants. No maturity.
  • Paid only out of production
  • Attached to the ground, so it survives insolvency
  • Leaves the cap table alone

What you actually own.

A fraction of a registered royalty, and the cash it throws off. Not equity. Not debt. A third thing that has never had a market.

Stapled to the licence

Registered against the ground, not the company.

Senior to equity

Paid off the top line, before costs and shareholders.

Runs for the reserve life

On long-life assets, decades. It passes to your heirs.

Tradeable

Exit the position without unwinding the royalty.

Risk

How you lose money on this.

Royalties are senior and durable. They are not safe. Anyone selling you mining without this page is selling you something else.

Production

If the mine stalls, floods, misses grade or loses its permit, the cash stops. Nothing accrues in the background. Nothing is owed to you.

Commodity price

A revenue royalty moves with the price of what's sold. A long downturn cuts every payment, and can shut a marginal mine entirely.

Jurisdiction

The extra return exists because these assets sit in harder countries. Licences get challenged. Enforcing a claim locally is slow and uncertain.

Liquidity

Transferable is not the same as liquid. Early on the book will be thin and spreads wide. Size every position as if you'll hold it to maturity.

Structure

You hold a fraction of a vehicle that holds the royalty. That's a legal layer between you and the cash. Digital-asset rules are still moving.

Counterparty

The operator has to report honestly and stay solvent. Registration is your protection. It's also a lawsuit, which means time and cost.

Someone explained royalty financing to me like it was obvious. A permanent cut of a mine's revenue. Ahead of the equity. No interest, no covenants, no repayment date. If the mine produces, you get paid. If it doesn't, nobody defaults.

Three companies quietly own that entire asset class. Not one of the royalties inside them has ever traded on its own. If you have $250,000 and a real view on copper, there is nothing for you to buy.

So we are not building a better royalty company. We are building the market underneath one. Royalties aren't new. A place to trade them is.

Two things say the timing is right. Questions

What serious people ask first.

What am I buying?
A fraction of a vehicle whose only asset is a royalty registered against a specific mining licence — plus your share of the cash it produces. Not shares in the mine. Not a loan to the operator. The token is the record, not the asset.
Is this a security?
Treat it as one. Depending on structure and where you live, it may be regulated. That's why access is restricted, why onboarding includes suitability checks, and why nothing here is an offer.
What if the mine stops producing?
The cash stops. Nothing accrues, nothing is owed, nothing accelerates. The claim stays on the licence, so a restart brings it back — but a suspended mine can pay nothing for years.
How is a royalty valued?
Net present value of the expected cash flow, across scenarios for production, price and discount rate. We price the risk-weighted case, not the operator's plan. The assumptions publish with the listing so you can substitute your own.
Why non-precious metals?
That's where the mispricing is. Gold is a quarter of mining and almost all of the royalty market, so precious royalties are bid to single digits. Copper, iron ore and zinc price several points wider — partly for real reasons, partly because the buyers never existed.
Can I sell before production starts?
That's the intent — and a pre-production royalty can appreciate as the asset de-risks. The caveat is honest: a market needs someone on the other side. Early on, expect a thin book.
What's the minimum?
Around $25,000 per listing, depending on the listing and your jurisdiction.
How does Midas get paid?
A structuring fee on the raise, paid by the operator, plus a fee on secondary trades. We don't mark up the royalty rate between the operator and the board.
GRR or NSR — does it matter?
A gross revenue royalty pays on everything sold. A net smelter return deducts transport and refining first. Usually they land close, but deductions are exactly where a royalty gets quietly worse than it looks. Every listing states which it is.
Why would an operator give up a perpetual royalty?
Because the alternative is worse. Equity sold at the bottom is permanent dilution on every tonne. Bank debt on a single-asset frontier mine often isn't available at any price. And they keep the whole cap table.
Where is Midas regulated?
The structure is being built in the UAE, with host-country counsel on each royalty. We picked it because the licensing path for tokenised real-world assets is workable there rather than theoretical. Specifics are in the disclosures.
Haven't platforms tried to crowdfund mining before?
They have, and they stalled on the same thing: nothing worth buying on one side, no capital on the other. We start from supply — royalties on assets that already produce — and go to investors who already understand the instrument.
